Evolutionary Biology
Understanding Bacterial Resistance Evolution
Antibiotic resistance evolves through mutation, selection, and gene transfer. The forecast above does not model these steps: it learns how the resistant share has moved from year to year in laboratory surveillance data.
Selection
Drug Pressure
Every course of an antibiotic kills susceptible cells and leaves resistant ones to grow. The more a drug is used, the faster the resistant share of a population rises, which is the trend the yearly lab results record.
HGT
Horizontal Gene Transfer
Bacteria exchange resistance genes via conjugation (plasmids), transformation (naked DNA), and transduction (phage). A plasmid spreading through a population can make the resistant share jump within a year or two, a change no trend model can see coming.
Surveillance
Yearly Lab Results
The model learns from BV-BRC laboratory results that carry a collection year, grouped per genus and antibiotic. A year counts only with at least 20 results, and years with under 30 are drawn hollow on the chart, since a handful of isolates can swing the share.
Genes
Resistance Genes
Each antibiotic class selects for characteristic resistance genes. Beta-lactams select for blaTEM/blaKPC; fluoroquinolones select for gyrA mutations and qnr genes; aminoglycosides for AMEs. Whether an isolate counts as resistant follows the clinical breakpoints from EUCAST / CLSI M100.

Clinical Implications & Antimicrobial Stewardship
De-escalation
Switch from broad-spectrum to narrow-spectrum antibiotics as soon as culture sensitivity data confirms susceptibility. This helps slow down resistance development.
Antibiotic Cycling
Rotating between drug classes at the population level can slow accumulation of resistance genes in the bacterial gene pool.
Combination Therapy
Using two synergistic antibiotics reduces the probability that a single mutation confers resistance to both agents simultaneously.
Optimal Duration
Shorter antibiotic courses reduce selective pressure, which slows the year-on-year rise the forecast tracks.
