Evolutionary Biology
Understanding Bacterial Resistance Evolution
Antibiotic resistance evolves through mutation, selection, and gene transfer. Our simulation models these population dynamics using logistic growth calibrated per antibiotic class.
Logistic
Population Growth Model
Resistance fraction follows a logistic S-curve: slow initial spread, exponential acceleration, then plateau. The model is parameterised per antibiotic using mutation speed, peak resistance, and sigmoid growth rate derived from published clinical data.
HGT
Horizontal Gene Transfer
Bacteria exchange resistance genes via conjugation (plasmids), transformation (naked DNA), and transduction (phage). Our model accounts for HGT acceleration by increasing the logistic growth rate when high-GC genomic regions (insertion sites) are detected.
Hotspots
Mutation Hotspot Detection
Genomic windows with elevated GC content and tandem repeat density are scored as mutation hotspots — these regions are where transposons and integrons insert resistance cassettes. The model identifies the top 10 highest-risk positions in your submitted genome.
Genes
Resistance Gene Activation
Each antibiotic class activates characteristic resistance genes over time. Beta-lactams select for blaTEM/blaKPC; fluoroquinolones select for gyrA mutations and qnr genes; aminoglycosides for AMEs. Activation timing reflects clinical breakpoint data from EUCAST / CLSI M100.

Clinical Implications & Antimicrobial Stewardship
De-escalation
Switch from broad-spectrum to narrow-spectrum antibiotics as soon as culture sensitivity data confirms susceptibility — slows resistance development.
Antibiotic Cycling
Rotating between drug classes at the population level can slow accumulation of resistance genes in the bacterial gene pool.
Combination Therapy
Using two synergistic antibiotics reduces the probability that a single mutation confers resistance to both agents simultaneously.
Optimal Duration
Shorter antibiotic courses reduce selective pressure. The timeline model helps identify the minimum effective treatment window before resistance emerges.
